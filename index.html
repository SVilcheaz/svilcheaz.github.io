C++, ROS2, PX4, Gazebo, Isaac ROS, Jetson Orin Nano

The first mission is autonomous flight to an assigned destination and approximate landing-pad location: build an obstacle map from onboard sensors, plan and follow a safe route, replan around new geometry, and land using a marker. The operator authorizes the mission; avoidance decisions come from perception. Phases 0–2 are complete, including the shared stereo/IMU rig and validated metric depth. Phase 3 is in progress, integrating visual navigation with PX4. Mapping, planning, mission recovery, and precision landing remain planned; semantic search follows in Phase 7.

Development roadmap

  1. Environment & bridge

    Completed

    Established the PC/Jetson deployment and networking: Gazebo Harmonic, PX4 SITL, QGroundControl, sensor bridge, and RViz2 on the PC; companion components and the XRCE-DDS Agent on the Jetson.

  2. Offboard flight control

    Completed

    Jetson-side C++ control of takeoff, waypoint flight, and landing in PX4/Gazebo, including a controller-loss recovery check.

  3. Stereo/IMU rig & metric obstacle sensing

    Completed

    Build a calibrated, synchronized stereo/IMU rig and compute metric depth on the Jetson with Isaac ROS CUDA SGM. Validate range, error, coverage, invalid data, latency, and resource use against known surfaces, then run alongside the completed hover/landing controller.

  4. Visual navigation & PX4 fusion

    In progress

    Use the shared rig for cuVSLAM visual-inertial odometry. Validate frames, timing, scale, and tracking health, then feed external vision through a C++ adapter into PX4 EKF2. Verify actual fusion in logs and complete a bounded flight with GNSS fusion disabled and a valid local estimate.

  5. 3D mapping, planning & replanning

    Planned

    Combine metric depth and capture-time pose in a bounded OctoMap of occupied, free, and unknown space. Use C++ 3D A* with vehicle clearance and geofence checks, then execute collision-checked segments within speed/acceleration limits through the existing Offboard interface. Replan around moved obstacles, fly only through observed-free space, and reject unreachable routes.

  6. Mission supervision & recovery

    Planned

    Extend the C++ supervisor from takeoff through navigation/replanning to landing and disarm. Set speed from validated free range, latency, and braking distance. Verify recovery during actual PX4 GNSS loss, stale depth, and localization loss/reset; invalidate unsafe trajectories and use the verified PX4 fallback when a valid position hold is unavailable.

  7. Integrated mission & precision landing

    Planned

    Add a calibrated downward camera and AprilTag relative-pose guidance with tag-loss handling. Demonstrate one continuous goal-to-landing mission, a changed obstacle layout without edited avoidance waypoints, and refusal/recovery when safe progress is unavailable. Measure touchdown offset against a predefined tolerance and confirm disarm.

  8. Semantic search mission

    Planned

    After reliable navigation, search for a specified object or person in a bounded area. Select the target class, detector, and response when this phase starts. Confirm detections across frames and use calibrated depth and pose to locate the target, while retaining obstacle avoidance, recovery, and a target-not-found outcome. YOLO and TensorRT are candidates, subject to measured need and resource limits.
